# Put the idle Windows PC to work

Send full test runs to a new, clean Linux space on your Windows PC, leave typechecks, lint and builds on the Mac for now, and rent nothing yet. Measured on 5 and 6 November 2026, the PC ran the real full suite (2,980 test files, 37,923 tests) in **232 and 237 seconds**, against 382 to 696 seconds on a calm Mac and 18 to 56 minutes on the busy Mac that night. Full test runs are the real load: about 90% of the Plan Queue's check time, while the AI tools were 1% to 10% of visible processor use. At 100 full runs a day the PC should cost about **£13 a month in electricity** (assumed, not measured), against **£187 to £258 a month** for the realistic cloud options, and each run needs only a small git snapshot: 312 changed files came to 3.7 MB in under 5 seconds. The catch is that 25 tests failed on Linux in every PC run, most from missing system packages, so a PC pass cannot replace a Mac pass yet. Cheaper runs matter as much as moved ones: dropping the simulated browser from non-browser tests cut their processor time by 43% in a trial. Whole agent sessions can follow later, in a virtual machine on the PC, as a branch and a plan with a separate login per machine. Avoid test code beside your logins, copied login files, automated Antigravity runs, client code on Noah's computers or shared cloud machines, and unfinished work pushed to a public repository.

**Update, 6 November 2026.** James has since decided: not to start yet; no sandbox for now, so the Linux space keeps access to Windows; no limit on how much of the PC tests can use; and a pass on the PC counts with no full Mac run. Where this report recommends otherwise, the [plan](/Users/suas/work/orchestrat0r/ai-orchestrator/docs/plans/2026-11-06-offload-heavy-work_plan.md) takes priority.

**How to read this report.** First-hand figures were measured on your machines on 5 and 6 November 2026. Those from the [draft offload plan](/Users/suas/work/orchestrat0r/ai-orchestrator/docs/plans/2026-11-06-offload-heavy-work_plan.md) come from the agent writing that plan; I checked its PC results against the raw logs, but not its Mac process shares or run counts. Prices were read on 5 November 2026, converted at that day's [ECB rates](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/eurofxref/eurofxref-daily.xml), and exclude VAT unless stated. **[older than 12 months]** means the source predates 5 November 2025; **[unverified]** means the notes could not confirm it; **[single report]** means one person's account; "undated" means true only as read on 5 November 2026. Quotes are near-exact. "My reading" or "my judgement" marks reasoning rather than a source.

## Full test runs move first because they are the load

| Work | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Full test suite | Clean Linux space on the PC | 1.6 to 3 times faster than a calm Mac, and the biggest load |
| Targeted tests, typechecks, lint, builds | Mac for now | Moving them saves only seconds |
| Signing, Keychain and other Mac-only checks | Mac | Cannot run elsewhere ([draft plan](/Users/suas/work/orchestrat0r/ai-orchestrator/docs/plans/2026-11-06-offload-heavy-work_plan.md)) |
| Whole agent sessions | Later, a Linux virtual machine on the PC | Not the main load |
| Checks on pushed ai-orchestrator code | GitHub's free runners | Free on a public repository, at tens of runs a day |
| Client repositories | Your own machines or a single-customer dedicated server | Trust decides this, not speed |
| Overflow | One rented server, only if needed | £216 to £258 a month |
| Noah's computers | Leave out for now | Nothing protects code from a machine's owner |

**Test runs are what saturates the Mac.** An average of 17.5 of its 18 cores were busy across a 45-minute sample, never below 15.9, with 37% of that operating-system time, and memory was not the limit ([Mac load samples, 5 to 6 Nov 2026](/Users/suas/work/orchestrat0r/ai-orchestrator/_scratch/offload-research/mac-load-samples.jsonl)). The PC, a 16-core Ryzen 9 9950X3D with 190 GB of memory, was about 12% busy. In three process snapshots, test programs were 33% to 63% of the Mac's visible processor use, against 1% to 10% for the AI tools ([draft plan, 6 Nov 2026](/Users/suas/work/orchestrat0r/ai-orchestrator/docs/plans/2026-11-06-offload-heavy-work_plan.md)). On 4 and 5 November, after removing cached replays, 60 real full test runs took 1,338 minutes of Plan Queue check time and 28 hit the time limit, while 432 other checks took about 150 minutes (same plan). Alone, a run never timed out; alongside two or more other checks, 13 of 17 did ([agent memory note, 5 Nov 2026](/Users/suas/.claude/projects/-Users-suas-work-orchestrat0r-ai-orchestrator/memory/plan-queue-checks-time-out-when-overlapping.md)).

A core-minute is one core busy for one minute; a worker is one parallel test process. PC rows are measured ([benchmark log](/Users/suas/work/orchestrat0r/ai-orchestrator/_scratch/offload-research/offload-bench-report.txt); [three-run log](/Users/suas/work/orchestrat0r/ai-orchestrator/_scratch/offload-research/offload-bench-lanes-report.txt), 5 to 6 Nov 2026). Mac rows are Vitest's own totals ([draft plan](/Users/suas/work/orchestrat0r/ai-orchestrator/docs/plans/2026-11-06-offload-heavy-work_plan.md)), which also count waiting, so the two are not exactly alike.

**The fair comparison is the calm Mac, where the PC is 1.6 to 3 times faster.** The 5 to 14 times against the busy Mac flatters the PC, because the test runner picks its worker count once at the start, and 22 of 36 logged Mac runs got a single worker (same plan). On the PC, going past 16 workers buys almost nothing. Three 8-worker runs started together all finished in 9 minutes 32 seconds with the same results as single runs and a peak load of 36, about **19 full runs an hour**.

**Benchmark scores would not have predicted this.** The M5 Max leads on single-thread speed by 16% to 50% ([Cputronic aggregator, undated](https://cputronic.com/en/cpu/compare/apple-m5-max-40-core-gpu-vs-amd-ryzen-9-9950x3d)). My reading, not tested directly, is that this job is mostly starting processes and touching small files, where macOS is slow: an M5 Max took 31.7 seconds for `pnpm install` against 7.9 for a Ryzen 9 9950X on Linux ([disk-perf table, 3 Jul 2026 rows, self-reported, different machines](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NullVoxPopuli/disk-perf-git-and-pnpm/main/README.md)). So time the real job before trusting estimates.

**Only the Linux side of the PC is worth using.** In the only same-machine tests found, native Windows took about 1.85 times as long as WSL2 (the Linux inside Windows) on a process-heavy build ([Memfault, 10 Oct 2024](https://interrupt.memfault.com/blog/comparing-fw-dev-envs) **[older than 12 months]**), and 2.9 to 3.9 times as long on `git clean` and `pnpm install`, on what appears to be the same machine ([disk-perf table, 9 Feb 2025 rows](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NullVoxPopuli/disk-perf-git-and-pnpm/main/README.md) **[older than 12 months]**). Files on the Windows drive reached from Linux were 28 to 250 times slower ([WSL #9430, Jan 2023 measurements](https://github.com/microsoft/WSL/issues/9430) **[older than 12 months]**), so code must live on the Linux disk. The suite has never run on native Windows ([draft plan](/Users/suas/work/orchestrat0r/ai-orchestrator/docs/plans/2026-11-06-offload-heavy-work_plan.md)), which should keep only the browser and Android work it already does.

**Typechecks, lint and builds gain too little to move yet.** The PC took 23 seconds for `tsc --noEmit`, 42 for the spec typecheck (a run that reported one real type error), 11 for lint, and 26 and 16 for the two builds ([benchmark log](/Users/suas/work/orchestrat0r/ai-orchestrator/_scratch/offload-research/offload-bench-report.txt)), against Plan Queue averages on the loaded Mac of 26, 49, 12, 36 and 21 ([draft plan](/Users/suas/work/orchestrat0r/ai-orchestrator/docs/plans/2026-11-06-offload-heavy-work_plan.md)). Sending them would save a few seconds and add about 5 for the snapshot.

**The catch: these timings are for runs that did not fully pass.** Across eight full PC runs, 25 tests failed every time and 4 only sometimes ([failure analysis, 6 Nov 2026](/Users/suas/work/orchestrat0r/ai-orchestrator/_scratch/offload-research/offload-bench-analysis.txt)). Of the 25, 15 are Electron not starting because 5 system libraries are missing and 6 are the missing `sqlite3` command ([failure breakdown, 6 Nov 2026](/Users/suas/work/orchestrat0r/ai-orchestrator/_scratch/offload-research/offload-bench-failures.txt)), which installing those packages should clear, though that is untested. Two are the push guard, where git 2.43 is suspected but unconfirmed, and 2 are not yet understood. Run the full suite on the same commit on both machines and compare before trusting any PC result (my judgement).

## A clean Linux space and a small snapshot do the moving

**Use a new Linux space, not the Ubuntu already on the PC.** That Ubuntu holds your `.ssh`, `.aws`, `.azure` and `.claude` folders and client code ([PC inventory, 5 Nov 2026](/Users/suas/work/orchestrat0r/ai-orchestrator/_scratch/offload-research/windows-inventory-2-report.txt)) and can read the whole C: drive, including your credentials folder ([draft plan](/Users/suas/work/orchestrat0r/ai-orchestrator/docs/plans/2026-11-06-offload-heavy-work_plan.md)). AI-written test code must not sit beside those. Microsoft says WSL "is not a security sandbox for running untrusted code" ([WSL security model, undated](https://wsl.dev/technical-documentation/security/)), while Hyper-V partitions, Windows' built-in virtual machines, "provide strong isolation boundaries" ([Microsoft, updated 16 Feb 2026](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows-server/virtualization/hyper-v/manage/manage-hyper-v-scheduler-types)). So the research's first choice on a Pro machine is a virtual machine. My judgement is to start with a new WSL space for ai-orchestrator's tests, because that code is public and the timings above were taken on WSL, and to build the virtual machine before any agent or client code goes near the PC. Its speed is unmeasured, and whether it starts with nobody logged in is untested ([Set-VM docs, Windows Server page, undated](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/powershell/module/hyper-v/set-vm)), so time the suite there and try one restart first.

**Plan for restarts.** WSL runs only inside a logged-in session: I found no Microsoft page documenting a start before logon ([WSL #9271, Dec 2022](https://github.com/microsoft/WSL/issues/9271) **[older than 12 months]**). The PC restarted four times between 14 and 24 October (my count from six log entries in the [PC inventory](/Users/suas/work/orchestrat0r/ai-orchestrator/_scratch/offload-research/windows-inventory-2-report.txt)), so recovery has to be automatic: power-on after mains loss, automatic logon, an at-logon task that starts the worker and Linux, and Tailscale set to run unattended ([Tailscale, Sep 2026](https://tailscale.com/kb/1088/run-unattended)).

**Games need a pause.** Processor caps do not work in Hyper-V on client Windows ([Microsoft, updated 16 Feb 2026](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows-server/virtualization/hyper-v/manage/manage-hyper-v-scheduler-types)) and WSL has no priority setting ([WSL #10136, open since May 2023](https://github.com/microsoft/WSL/issues/10136) **[older than 12 months]**), so freeze the work from inside Linux during games ([kernel docs, undated](https://docs.kernel.org/admin-guide/cgroup-v2.html)) (my judgement).

**Send a snapshot, not a continuous sync.** Build a commit with a [temporary index](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/git/git/master/Documentation/git.adoc), so your real staging area is never touched ([public script](https://gist.github.com/matta/a63a9d276dbf0445c36bcd3bece0071c)), then send only the new objects. The notes proposed a [git bundle](https://git-scm.com/docs/git-bundle), but a bundle refuses a bare commit hash, so the transfer uses a git pack, tested in a [throwaway repository](/Users/suas/work/orchestrat0r/ai-orchestrator/_scratch/offload-research/pack-transfer-test.sh) (first-hand, 6 Nov 2026). That gives one exact fingerprint for the code tested. rsync takes no snapshot of a live tree ([OneUpTime, Sep 2026](https://oneuptime.com/blog/post/2026-09-28-rsync-small-files-efs-metadata-round-trips/view)), Mutagen has had [no release since 24 Feb 2025](https://github.com/mutagen-io/mutagen/releases), and SSHFS was about 21 times slower even with no real network ([Voit, 2017](https://karl-voit.at/2017/07/26/sshfs-performance) **[older than 12 months]**). A snapshot follows `.gitignore`, so generated files a test needs will not travel, though that also keeps `.env` files out.

**Let each machine build its own `node_modules`.** The PC did a fresh clone in 7 seconds and a clean `npm ci` in 59 ([benchmark log](/Users/suas/work/orchestrat0r/ai-orchestrator/_scratch/offload-research/offload-bench-report.txt)), so set-up is cheap. Installing is also the riskiest step: Shai-Hulud 2.0 ran during package installation and harvested credentials ([Wiz, Nov 2025](https://www.wiz.io/blog/shai-hulud-2-0-ongoing-supply-chain-attack)). Limit install scripts with [npm's allow-list settings](https://docs.npmjs.com/cli/v11/using-npm/config) and keep credentials out of the space.

**Connect over SSH for now.** The notes leaned towards Harness's worker link, to keep a single way in, but its command route [refuses `node` and Python](/Users/suas/work/orchestrat0r/ai-orchestrator/src/main/app/run-on-node-exec-policy.ts) and [stops at 120 seconds and 96 KB of output](/Users/suas/work/orchestrat0r/ai-orchestrator/src/main/remote-node/node-control-rpc-schemas.ts). The draft plan chooses SSH because it needs no Harness rebuild, has nothing to deploy to the worker, and works when Harness is not running ([draft plan](/Users/suas/work/orchestrat0r/ai-orchestrator/docs/plans/2026-11-06-offload-heavy-work_plan.md)).

**A result must say exactly what it is.** Tie every run to its code fingerprint, command, settings, lockfile, Node version and platform, as the [Remote Execution API, v2.12.0, 12 Feb 2026](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bazelbuild/remote-apis/main/build/bazel/remote/execution/v2/remote_execution.proto) does. Keep "the tests failed" separate from "we could not find out", as [distcc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/distcc/distcc/master/man/distcc.1) does with reserved exit codes. Send only commands that change nothing: `vitest -u`, `eslint --fix` and installs stay on the Mac. Falling back to the Mac must obey the same limits, or one PC outage restarts every job on the overloaded machine (my judgement). No scheduler product is justified: Nomad expects [three or five servers (v2.1.1, 29 Oct 2026)](https://developer.hashicorp.com/nomad/docs/architecture), and the repo has [rejected a second task ledger](/Users/suas/work/orchestrat0r/ai-orchestrator/docs/decisions/rejected/privacy-and-architecture.md). Stripe's first version took under a day, and the team around it grew to about a dozen engineers ([Elhage, May 2024](https://blog.nelhage.com/post/stripe-dev-environment/) **[older than 12 months]**): reliability is where the cost goes.

## Cheaper, rarer runs beat more machines: one trial cut processor time 43%

This project started **382 test runs on 5 November, about 47 of them full or nearly full**, and the main folder's daily test runs rose from 54 to 276 in six days ([draft plan](/Users/suas/work/orchestrat0r/ai-orchestrator/docs/plans/2026-11-06-offload-heavy-work_plan.md)). Moving runs leaves that work in place, and a free PC invites more of it. The research ranks the savings as: reuse a pass on identical code, run the full suite once per finished job, run only affected tests while working, make each run cheaper, then TypeScript 7. No reuse rate has been measured, while the trial below is real, so I would put cheaper runs first (my judgement).

**Drop the simulated browser where nothing needs it.** At 16 workers on the PC, tests themselves were about 35% of the summed time and building the simulated browser about 27% ([benchmark log](/Users/suas/work/orchestrat0r/ai-orchestrator/_scratch/offload-research/offload-bench-report.txt)), because both test projects use jsdom, a simulated browser, with a fresh process per file ([vitest.config.ts](/Users/suas/work/orchestrat0r/ai-orchestrator/vitest.config.ts)). A first-hand trial on a throwaway copy of the `main` project (2,511 of the 2,980 files, 16 workers) shows the prize:

| Setting | Time | Processor time | Files failing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Today's setting (jsdom, fresh start per file) | 3 min 11 s | 2,775 seconds | 14 |
| Real `node` environment | 2 min 37 s | 1,575 seconds, **43% less** | 21 |
| Per-file isolation off | 1 min 47 s | 646 seconds, 77% less | 379 |

Sources: [benchmark log](/Users/suas/work/orchestrat0r/ai-orchestrator/_scratch/offload-research/offload-bench-report.txt) and, for the `node` row, the [second log](/Users/suas/work/orchestrat0r/ai-orchestrator/_scratch/offload-research/offload-bench-2-report.txt), 6 Nov 2026. The `node` change costs only 7 extra failing files, which can keep the simulated browser, and would save about a third of a whole run's processor time on any machine (my arithmetic). The `--environment` flag had no effect, so the config itself has to change. Turning isolation off shows how much of each run is set-up, but with 379 files failing it is not something to switch on.

**Reuse a pass when the code is identical.** Turborepo and Angular CLI added cache sharing across git worktrees in 2026 ([Turborepo 2.8, 23 Jan 2026](https://turborepo.dev/blog/2-8); [Angular CLI 22.1.0, 29 Jul 2026](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/angular/angular-cli/main/CHANGELOG.md)). A small record saying "this exact code passed this exact check" is simpler. The Plan Queue already keeps one, which stored timeouts and replayed them against unchanged code ([agent memory note, 5 Nov 2026](/Users/suas/.claude/projects/-Users-suas-work-orchestrat0r-ai-orchestrator/memory/plan-queue-checks-time-out-when-overlapping.md)), so store only clean passes. One changed file misses the whole suite, and how often two agents share a tree is unknown. The plan leaves this out for now, after its independent review: a test that fails only sometimes could get a lucky pass kept for good, and a separate record of passes would sit outside Harness. If it proves worth measuring, it belongs in the Plan Queue's existing cache.

**Run less of the suite while working.** `AGENTS.md` already reserves the full suite for the final check; enforcing that is the cheapest change here. Vitest's [`--changed`](https://vitest.dev/config/changed) runs only affected tests but misses code reached through dynamic imports ([CLI docs, v5.1.0](https://vitest.dev/guide/cli)). Keep a full run before merge.

**TypeScript 7 can wait.** Microsoft's 8 to 12 times is clock time ([TypeScript 7.0, 8 Jul 2026](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/typescript/announcing-typescript-7-0/)), and Angular's build stays on TypeScript 6 for now ([Angular blog, feed timestamp 25 Sep 2026, read through RSS](https://blog.angular.dev/an-update-on-angulars-typescript-7-powered-compiler-9619a35e2b0a)). A first try on the PC stopped at once on two removed settings in `tsconfig.electron.json` ([benchmark log](/Users/suas/work/orchestrat0r/ai-orchestrator/_scratch/offload-research/offload-bench-report.txt)). With type checks under a minute, the prize is small.

**Correction, 6 November 2026: skip the Spotlight change this report first suggested.** `~/work` already has a `.metadata_never_index` file and Spotlight holds nothing from it, so `.worktrees` and `_scratch` are already out of the index. See "Spotlight isn't the problem here" in the [plan](/Users/suas/work/orchestrat0r/ai-orchestrator/docs/plans/2026-11-06-offload-heavy-work_plan.md).

## Whole agent sessions move as a branch and a plan

**Move agents second, because on the evidence so far they are not what is hurting the Mac.** The 1% to 10% share comes from three snapshots that undercount short-lived programs, and nobody has published figures for processor use while waiting. The bigger danger is starvation: on 1 July three sessions running full test suites, plus reviewers and loop agents, drove load to about 290, and Harness's own watchdogs killed healthy agents ([agent memory note, 1 Jul 2026](/Users/suas/.claude/projects/-Users-suas-work-orchestrat0r-ai-orchestrator/memory/host-overload-watchdog-scaling.md)). Moving tests removes that cause.

**Keep Harness on the Mac and treat other machines as hosts.** That looks like where the field is going (my reading). Conductor's cloud shuts on 20 November ([announcement, undated; sign-ups closed 4 Nov 2026](https://www.conductor.build/announcement)) and the Vibe Kanban company closed ([10 Apr 2026](https://vibekanban.com/blog/shutdown)), while the Codex app drives hosts over SSH ([Remote connections, undated](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/remote-connections)). That is vendor documentation; no independent report of daily use was found.

**Their home should be Linux, not native Windows.** Harness can already run a whole agent session on a paired worker ([draft plan](/Users/suas/work/orchestrat0r/ai-orchestrator/docs/plans/2026-11-06-offload-heavy-work_plan.md)), but "On native Windows, Claude Code runs commands unsandboxed" ([Claude Code sandboxing, undated](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sandboxing)), on the account that holds your logged-in browser. A default WSL agent can reach those Windows files too ([WSL security model, undated](https://wsl.dev/technical-documentation/security/)), which is the argument for a virtual machine. A Linux worker exists in Harness's code but has never run for real, and every paired worker is fully trusted (same plan).

**Vendors' cloud agents take load off the Mac, but Claude's and Codex's add no model capacity, and the others are metered or unclear.**

| Product | Cheapest plan | Paid from the flat plan? | Machine | Code must be on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Claude Code cloud sessions](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/cloud-environments) (undated) | [Pro, $20 a month](https://claude.com/pricing) | Yes, no machine charge | 4 virtual cores, 16 GB | GitHub, or an upload of tracked changes under 100 MB |
| [Codex cloud](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/environments/cloud-environments.md) (undated) | [Plus, $20 a month](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/pricing) | Yes; cloud may use more of the allowance | Plus 2 cores, 8 GiB; Pro and Business 4 cores, 16 GiB | GitHub only |
| [Cursor Cloud Agents](https://cursor.com/docs/cloud-agent) (undated) | [Premium, $30 a month](https://cursor.com/pricing) | Unclear | Unpublished | GitHub, GitLab, Bitbucket, Azure DevOps |
| [GitHub Copilot cloud agent](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/concepts/agents/coding-agent/about-coding-agent) (undated) | [Pro, $10 a month](https://github.blog/changelog/2026/04/27/github-copilot-is-moving-to-usage-based-billing/) | No: AI credits plus a compute meter | Unpublished | GitHub only |

Devin has its own allowance with extra usage at API prices ([pricing, undated](https://devin.ai/pricing)), Google's Jules ends on 31 December ([changelog, 29 Oct 2026](https://jules.google/docs/changelog/)), and Antigravity has no cloud execution yet ([docs, undated](https://antigravity.google/docs/getting-started)). Claude's sessions "share rate limits with all other Claude and Claude Code usage" ([Cloud sessions, undated](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/claude-code-on-the-web)), no documented way to read their status was found, and the machine "may stop tasks that need significantly more memory" ([Cloud environments, undated](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/cloud-environments)). Nearly all these products need work pushed to a git host, which for ai-orchestrator means publishing unfinished work. Hosted sandboxes fit no better: about $0.22 to $0.68 an hour for 4 virtual cores and 8 GB ([Vercel, 10 Sep 2026](https://vercel.com/docs/vercel-sandbox/pricing)), built for short runs.

**Give each machine its own login.** Your October incident shows the risk, even on one machine: a sandboxed session could read but not write the Keychain, so a spent single-use login token stayed there, was reused, and the server revoked the whole login ([agent memory note, 17 Oct 2026](/Users/suas/.claude/projects/-Users-suas-work-orchestrat0r-ai-orchestrator/memory/claude-account-logout-at-refresh.md)). Copying a login to a second machine invites the same clash (my inference; no vendor states it for Claude). OpenAI does state it: "Do not share the same file across concurrent jobs or multiple machines" ([CI/CD auth guide, undated](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/auth/ci-cd-auth)). For Claude, `claude setup-token` gives a one-year token for scripts, though not for Remote Control, and a stray `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` silently moves scripted runs to API billing ([Authentication, undated](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/authentication)). For Codex, use device-code login on each machine, a beta that must be switched on in ChatGPT security settings ([Authentication, undated](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/auth)).

**On my reading the terms allow your own machines, with open doubts.** Anthropic's restrictions do not "prevent an end user from signing in to the unmodified Claude Code binary with their own Claude subscription, including where a platform hosts Claude Code" ([Legal and compliance, undated](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance)), though that wording is about hosting platforms and carries conditions. Three doubts remain: dozens of sessions at once may not be the "ordinary, individual usage" the limits assume; Harness handing a stored token to a remote process could be read as intermediating credentials, which a separate login per machine avoids; and the UK consumer terms say "You agree not to use our Services for any commercial or business purposes" ([Consumer Terms, effective 8 Oct 2025, still the live version](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/consumer-terms) **[older than 12 months]**), which is ambiguous but sits badly with client work. A Team plan, from $20 a seat a month billed annually or $25 monthly for two or more seats, would put client work under Commercial Terms ([Claude pricing, undated, read by a helper](https://claude.com/pricing)). Also watch `--bare`: Anthropic says it will become the default for scripted runs, and "bare mode doesn't use your subscription login" ([Non-interactive mode, undated](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/headless)). Harness starts Claude in that scripted mode ([draft plan](/Users/suas/work/orchestrat0r/ai-orchestrator/docs/plans/2026-11-06-offload-heavy-work_plan.md)).

**Moving a live session does not work, so move the job.** Nothing moves a running turn. Claude's transcript format "changes between versions" ([Sessions, undated](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sessions)) and its prompt cache is "effectively scoped to one machine and directory" ([Prompt caching, undated](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/prompt-caching.md)), so a moved session will probably use more of your limit (my reading). For Plan Queue work the right unit is the job: a plan document and a branch, started fresh on the other machine.

## At 100 runs a day, renting costs £187 to £258 a month against about £13 for the PC

| Option | 100 full runs a day | 300 full runs a day | Worth knowing |

**Client code stays on machines you control:** your own, or a single-customer dedicated server with full-disk encryption. On my reading of the [ICO's guide, undated](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/personal-information-what-is-it/what-is-personal-information-a-guide/), source code with no personal data is not personal data, but client contracts, NDAs and subcontractor clauses still apply, and none were read. Real data hides in fixtures, dumps, logs and old git history. If it is there, the hosting company becomes a subcontractor under data protection law, and the client must have authorised that in writing ([UK GDPR Article 28(2)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/28)). Scan any repository that could hold children's or SEND records before it moves. This is not legal advice.

## Conclusion

The useful surprise is that the Mac's problem is not a shortage of machines. It has faster cores and still lost to Linux on a quiet desktop, then drowned because nothing limits how many full runs start at once. The PC fixes tonight's overload but not that habit, so limits on concurrent runs, cheaper runs and result reuse are what will make the relief last. Four things would change this advice: if the push guard or the two unexplained failures prove to be real platform differences, a Mac run stays the final word; if the Mac is still overloaded once tests have moved, agents move sooner; if restarts cost real time, the virtual machine comes forward; and if the PC is unavailable for hours each week, a rented server does.

Still unmeasured: virtual machine speed, whole-PC power draw, whether installing the missing packages clears the failures, whether Tailscale reaches a WSL space, and how much relief the Mac gets. The research had limits too: its web search allowance ran out, OpenAI's terms were read only through an unofficial mirror, and no client contract was checked.
